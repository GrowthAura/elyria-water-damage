Should You Call a Plumber or a Restoration Crew First?

If you can shut off the water yourself, call the restoration crew first to start removing water and drying. If you cannot stop the water, call whoever can stop it fastest.

Shutting the main valve stops most leaks. Once water is off, the damage keeps growing only because water is sitting in floors and walls, which is a restoration job.

What a Plumber Fixes

  • Burst and leaking pipes
  • Water heater replacement
  • Drain and sewer line clogs
  • Toilets, faucets and fixtures
  • Backwater valves and sump pump installs

What a Restoration Crew Fixes

  • Standing water removal
  • Wet carpet, pad, drywall and insulation
  • Structural drying and moisture monitoring
  • Sewage cleanup and disinfection
  • Mold prevention and remediation
  • Insurance documentation

When You Need Both

  • Burst pipe: plumber repairs the pipe; crew dries the house. See burst pipe water damage.
  • Floor drain backup: drain service clears the line; crew cleans and disinfects. See basement floor drain backup.
  • Water heater failure: plumber replaces the tank; crew dries the basement.

In every case, starting the drying early is what keeps costs and mold down. See emergency water extraction and water mitigation.

Who To Call for Water Damage: Quick Decision Guide

  • Water still flowing and you cannot stop it: call a plumber or shut the main valve, then call a restoration crew.
  • Water stopped, but floors and walls are wet: call a restoration crew first.
  • Water coming up from a floor drain: call a drain service to clear the line and a restoration crew to clean up.
  • Sewage in the house: call a restoration crew for Category 3 cleanup and a drain service for the blockage.
  • Ceiling leak from an ice dam: call a restoration crew for the inside and a roofer for the outside.
  • Sump pump failed: call a restoration crew for pump-out and a plumber or waterproofer for the pump.

Plumber or Water Damage Company: Why Order Matters

Plumbers are experts at pipes, but most do not carry extraction equipment, commercial dehumidifiers or moisture mapping tools. If you wait for the plumber to finish before calling for drying, water keeps soaking into walls and floors. Calling both at the same time saves hours, and hours matter in the first day.

Who Fixes a Leak vs Cleans It Up

The person who fixes the leak stops the cause. The person who cleans up stops the damage. Insurance claims often need records from both: the plumber's invoice shows the cause, and the restoration crew's moisture logs show the damage and the drying. Ask both for written documentation.

What Happens if You Only Call a Plumber

The pipe gets fixed, but water stays in drywall, insulation, carpet pad and subfloor. Within a few days, odors start, baseboards swell and mold can begin. Weeks later, paint bubbles or floors cup. At that point the repair is larger and may not be covered if the damage looks gradual.

Water Damage Restoration vs Plumber: Typical Response Times

Plumbers often schedule repairs and may not be able to come out at night. Restoration crews are set up for 24/7 emergency response because water damage grows every hour. In many cases, the restoration crew arrives first, stops or isolates the water and starts drying, and the plumber arrives later to make the permanent repair.

Do Restoration Crews Fix Pipes?

Most restoration crews do not perform plumbing repairs. They can stop the flow by closing valves and capping lines when needed, but the repair is done by a plumber. Some jobs also need an electrician, roofer or waterproofing contractor. The restoration crew handles the water, drying, cleanup and documentation.

What Each Trade Gives Your Insurer

  • Plumber: invoice and notes on the cause, such as a split pipe or failed fitting.
  • Restoration crew: photos, moisture maps, drying logs and a scope of damage.

Together, these show what happened and what was done about it.

Common Elyria Scenarios

A frozen pipe bursts in January: shut the main valve, call the restoration crew to start drying, then the plumber to repair the line. A floor drain backs up in a summer storm: stop using water, call the crew for cleanup and a drain service to clear the line. A water heater fails: shut the valve and gas, call the crew to dry the basement and the plumber to replace the tank.

The Cost of Waiting

Every hour of delay lets water travel farther into walls and floors. Calling a restoration crew right away keeps the drying job small. Waiting for the plumber first can turn a drying job into a tear-out job.

Can a Plumber Dry My House?

Some plumbing companies offer water removal, but most focus on repairs. Drying a house to normal moisture levels requires commercial dehumidifiers, air movers, moisture meters and daily monitoring. Ask whether the company will map moisture and provide drying records. If not, call a restoration crew for the drying part.

When You Only Need a Plumber

If a small drip was caught right away, nothing soaked, and the area is dry to the touch, you may only need a plumber. When in doubt, a quick moisture check confirms whether walls and floors are truly dry.

FAQs

Do I call a plumber or a restoration company?

Stop the water, then call a restoration crew to start drying. Call a plumber for the repair.

Yes. Both can work the same day.
