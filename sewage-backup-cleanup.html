Why Does Sewage Back Up Into Elyria Basements During Storms?

During heavy rain, city sewer lines can fill faster than they drain. Water and sewage then push back up through the lowest opening in the house, usually the basement floor drain.

Parts of Elyria's older sewer network carry both storm water and household waste, and many homes connect through clay tile or cast iron laterals that are decades old. Tree roots, grease, cracked pipe and sagging sections all slow the flow. Put a summer thunderstorm or fast snowmelt on top of that, and basement drains become the overflow point. Sewer backup in Elyria, OH also happens without rain when a lateral is blocked by roots, wipes or grease, and a toilet or shower drain overflows.

Why You Should Not Clean Up Sewage Yourself

Sewage is Category 3 water under the IICRC S500 standard. It can carry bacteria, viruses and parasites that make people sick through skin contact, splashes or breathing contaminated mist. Children, older adults and anyone with a weak immune system are at higher risk. Bleach and a mop do not reach the sewage that soaked into drywall, wood and carpet backing. Safe cleanup means protective equipment, controlled removal and disinfection of everything the water touched.

Until the crew arrives:

  • Stay out of the water and keep kids and pets away
  • Do not run water, flush toilets or use the washer, which adds more to the backup
  • Turn off power to the basement if you can reach the panel from a dry spot
  • Open windows if it is safe, and close the basement door
  • Take photos for insurance from the stairs

Our Category 3 Sewage Cleanup Process

Step 1. Protection. Crew members wear protective suits, gloves, boots and respirators. The work area is separated from the rest of the house.

Step 2. Removal of sewage and water. Pumps and extraction equipment remove liquids and solids.

Step 5. Disinfection. Antimicrobial products are applied to every surface in the affected area.

Step 6. Drying. Dehumidifiers and air movers dry the structure, and readings are taken daily.

Step 7. Odor control. HEPA air filtration and deodorization remove lingering smells.

What Has To Be Thrown Out After a Sewage Backup?

  • Carpet and carpet pad: always removed
  • Drywall and paneling: cut out above the water line
  • Insulation in wet walls: removed
  • Upholstered furniture and mattresses: usually discarded
  • Cardboard boxes, paper and fabric items: usually discarded
  • Hard items like metal, glass and sealed plastics: cleaned and disinfected

Preventing the Next Sewer Backup

  • Install a backwater valve on the main sewer line
  • Have the lateral camera-inspected for roots and cracks
  • Never flush wipes, even ones labeled flushable
  • Keep grease out of kitchen drains
  • Plug or cap unused basement drains

If water is coming up through your floor drain during storms, see basement floor drain backup.

Signs of a Sewer Line Problem Before a Backup

Many sewer backups give warnings first. Watch for:

  • Gurgling from drains or toilets when other fixtures run
  • Slow drains in more than one fixture
  • Water rising in the basement floor drain when the washer drains
  • Sewer smell near the floor drain or cleanout
  • Wet or sinking spots in the yard over the sewer lateral

If you notice these, have a drain service camera-inspect the line. Fixing roots or a cracked lateral early is far cheaper than a basement sewage cleanup.

Sewage Backup Cleanup FAQs

Is sewage in the basement dangerous?

Yes. Sewage can carry harmful bacteria and viruses. Avoid contact, keep people and pets away and let a trained crew handle cleanup.

Who cleans up a sewer backup?

A water damage restoration crew trained in Category 3 cleanup. A plumber or the city handles the blockage, and the restoration crew handles the damage inside the home.

Can carpet be saved after a sewage backup?

No. Carpet and pad touched by sewage are removed because they cannot be fully disinfected.

How long does sewage cleanup take?

Removal and cleaning usually take one to two days, followed by three to five days of drying.

What about a toilet overflow cleanup?

If the water came from the bowl after flushing, it is treated as contaminated. Small overflows on tile can be cleaned quickly; overflows that reached carpet or ran through the ceiling need full Category 3 cleanup.
